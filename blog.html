Curtain Wall vs. Window Wall: Which System Fits Chennai High-Rises?

When engineering commercial towers along Mount Road or OMR, choosing between continuous curtain walls and floor-to-floor window walls defines the project's structural budget and long-term acoustic seal. Curtain walls span floor slabs externally, eliminating thermal bridging and accommodating live structural deflections. Window walls, while lower in initial cost, introduce multiple slab-edge joints prone to water penetration.

Fire Safety & Codes • 5 Min Read

National Building Code (NBC 2016) Fire-Rated ACP Cladding Guidelines

Recent high-rise incidents underscore why untreated low-density polyethylene (LDPE) core ACP panels are prohibited on commercial facades above 15 meters. NBC 2016 mandates Class A2 or Class B1 fire-retardant mineral-filled cores. We explain vertical fire stops at every floor level and third-party laboratory flammability test compliance required by CMDA.

Coastal Durability • 7 Min Read

Designing Coastal Glass Facades: Mitigating Cyclone Pressures & Salt

Chennai's coastline experiences extreme humidity, airborne salinity, and cyclonic wind gusts exceeding 200 km/h (IS 875 Basic Wind Speed Vb = 50 m/s). Aluminum mullions require minimum 25-micron architectural anodizing or Class 2 PVDF coating to resist pitting corrosion. Structural silicone must be specified for high dynamic movement accommodation.

Energy Efficiency • 5 Min Read

Double Glazed Units (DGU) vs. Single Glass: Thermal Heat Reduction

In Chennai's intense tropical climate, single clear glass acts as a greenhouse, inflating commercial HVAC energy consumption. High-performance DGU featuring a 12mm argon-filled spacer with soft-coat Low-E glass achieves a Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC) of under 0.25, blocking infrared radiation while allowing 50%+ natural daylight.

Point-supported glass facades create grand, column-free entrances. The primary engineering challenge is calculating out-of-plane lateral wind buckling for the structural glass fins. Preventing Water Ingress: Rainscreen Pressure Equalization Principles

Water enters facades when three conditions coincide: water presence, an opening, and a driving force (air pressure differential). By applying the rear-ventilated rainscreen principle with baffled weep holes, Wallmark neutralizes wind-driven air pressure, ensuring zero water ingress even during intense coastal storms.
